# Weekly petroleum inventories

The Economy desk's [energy read](https://austinmermans.github.io/public-record/#energy) uses two keyless files from the U.S. Energy Information Administration's Weekly Petroleum Status Report: [Table 4 CSV](https://ir.eia.gov/wpsr/table4.csv) for the reported current, prior and comparable-year stock levels, and [commercial crude JSON](https://ir.eia.gov/wpsr/psw00.json) for the chart. The [official release schedule](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/schedule.php) is linked, but a scheduled date is not treated as a published result. EIA data is public domain; attribution and publication date stay beside the figures. Public Record's original page, prose and software retain their separate licenses.

The selected Table 4 rows are **Commercial (Excluding SPR)**, **Total Motor Gasoline** and **Distillate Fuel Oil**. They are US inventory stocks in million barrels, not barrels/day production, refinery throughput, consumption, exports or oil prices. The collector checks unique row identity, date headings, each displayed weekly difference, published year-ago percentage within its one-decimal rounding, and the crude current/prior levels against the thousand-barrel JSON series. Raw official bytes and SHA-256 receipts are retained; the build replays normalization against them. An unverified or failed refresh retains the last good capture as stale rather than presenting new results.

The chart is the JSON feed's **current six-year rolling edition** of commercial crude stocks excluding the Strategic Petroleum Reserve. It is not a collection of original weekly report vintages. The year-ago Table 4 date is the publisher's comparable week, which may differ from exactly 52 weeks. The page does not transform inventory changes into a price forecast, demand estimate or trading signal. It reports crude and product directions separately; a divergent week is not one composite supply/demand assessment. There is no analyst-consensus expectation or release surprise in this increment.

Collection requests are serial and limited to two official files per scheduled refresh, with an identifying project User-Agent. Refresh attempts run with the publication workflow twice daily, but new EIA observations follow the publisher's weekly release cadence. EIA's [automated-retrieval policy](https://www.eia.gov/about/privacy_security_policy.php) warns that excessive robot activity may be blocked; this collector does not poll intraday. The independent per-channel change ledger distinguishes a new report week from a change to a value within the same week. A first capture is a baseline, not a news event.
