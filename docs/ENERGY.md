# Weekly petroleum inventories

The Economy desk's [energy read](https://austinmermans.github.io/public-record/#energy) uses five keyless EIA pages/files: [Table 4 CSV](https://ir.eia.gov/wpsr/table4.csv) for the current, prior and comparable-year stock levels; [commercial crude JSON](https://ir.eia.gov/wpsr/psw00.json), [motor-gasoline history](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/hist/LeafHandler.ashx?n=PET&s=WGTSTUS1&f=W) and [distillate history](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/hist/LeafHandler.ashx?n=PET&s=WDISTUS1&f=W) for the three charts; and the [official release schedule](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/schedule.php) for expected calendar entries. A scheduled date is never treated as a published result. EIA data is public domain; attribution and publication date stay beside the figures. Public Record's original page, prose and software retain their separate licenses.

The selected Table 4 rows are **Commercial (Excluding SPR)**, **Total Motor Gasoline** and **Distillate Fuel Oil**. They are US inventory stocks in million barrels, not barrels/day production, refinery throughput, consumption, exports or oil prices. The collector checks unique row identity, date headings, each displayed weekly difference, published year-ago percentage within its one-decimal rounding, and the current/prior levels against the corresponding thousand-barrel histories. Product history's year-ago week is also reconciled when that exact observation is present. Raw official bytes and SHA-256 receipts are retained; the build replays normalization against them. A core stock failure retains the last good capture as stale; an individual history or schedule failure does not stop verified current stocks from updating, but removes that enhancement and marks its gap.

Crude history is the JSON feed's **current six-year rolling edition**. Gasoline and distillate histories come from EIA's current weekly series pages, reaching back to 1990 and 1982 respectively. Six weekly observations are absent across four gaps in early 1982–83 distillate history; the chart breaks its line at those gaps. None of these histories reconstructs original weekly report vintages. The year-ago Table 4 date is the publisher's comparable week, which may differ from exactly 52 weeks. A same-season reference compares the latest stock with the closest reporting Friday to that calendar date in each of the prior five years (at most three days away); its five exact source weeks and values are expandable beside the claim. February 29 anchors use February 28 in non-leap comparison years. This is five individual observations, not a model, demand-normalized level or price signal. The page reports crude and product directions separately; a divergent week is not one composite supply/demand assessment. There is no analyst-consensus expectation or release surprise in this increment.

Calendar entries are date-only because EIA says its CSV tables appear **after** the scheduled time, not necessarily at that minute. The ordinary window is after Wednesday 10:30 a.m. Eastern; published holiday exceptions replace the date/time where specified. The calendar shows up to eight future reporting weeks under the currently published schedule but does not project into a calendar year for which EIA has not yet posted any holiday exceptions. EIA may revise future exceptions; daily refresh rechecks the page. If this boundary leaves no future entry, the schedule is marked unavailable until EIA updates its page. Dates are expected releases, not confirmation that a report arrived.

Collection requests are serial and limited to five official pages/files per scheduled refresh, with an identifying project User-Agent. Refresh attempts run with the publication workflow twice daily, but new EIA observations follow the publisher's weekly release cadence. EIA's [automated-retrieval policy](https://www.eia.gov/about/privacy_security_policy.php) warns that excessive robot activity may be blocked; this collector does not poll intraday. The independent per-channel change ledger distinguishes a new report week from a change to a value within the same week, and can flag corrections in all three captured histories. A first capture is a baseline, not a news event.
